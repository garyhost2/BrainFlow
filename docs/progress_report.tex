\section{Objective and the governing constraint}
% ===========================================================================

I reconstruct viewed images from single-trial fMRI in the Natural Scenes
Dataset~\cite{allen2022nsd} (7T, $1.8$\,mm, \texttt{nsdgeneral} ROI,
$V\!\approx\!13$--$16$k voxels; subjects 1, 2, 5, 7). The outer pipeline follows
MindEye2~\cite{scotti2024mindeye2}: a frozen unCLIP/SDXL decoder consumes a
$256\times1664$ ViT-bigG token sequence, and the learned component is the
\emph{prior} that maps brain activity onto that sequence.

One fact governs every decision below: there are $\approx\!27$k training trials
per subject. This is a severely data-limited regime for a model of this size,
and it is the reason the work has converged on \emph{conditioning} the
generative problem rather than enlarging it.

\section{The chain of elimination}
% ===========================================================================

The current design was not chosen in advance. Each of the following results
removed a class of explanation and narrowed what remained. I present them in
that order because the argument for the present method is cumulative.

\begin{finding}{F1 --- Pixel-space targets are not learnable from fMRI.}
My first architecture ran conditional flow matching~\cite{lipman2023flow,
liu2023rectified} over Stable-Diffusion VAE latents. It plateaued at PixCorr
$0.12$--$0.15$. A variant that derived the flow source from the conditioning
collapsed outright: the training loss fell $0.70\!\to\!0.095$ in five epochs
while reconstruction quality \emph{fell}, because the model drove the source
onto the target and made the velocity identically zero.
\emph{Consequence:} move the learning problem into a semantic space, and treat
source degeneracy as a first-class hazard --- a lemma I return to in F8.
\end{finding}

\begin{finding}{F2 --- The token sequence admits neither a flat nor a spherical model.}
bigG tokens are not unit-normalised, and the decoder consumes raw values.
Modelling $X$ as a flat Gaussian ignores that CLIP semantics are carried by
\emph{direction} --- every downstream metric is a cosine --- and lets the flow
leave the thin shell where real tokens live. Normalising the sequence destroys
the magnitudes the decoder requires, irrecoverably.
\emph{Consequence:} neither standard geometry is admissible; I introduced the
polar reparametrisation of \S\ref{sec:method}, which is the contribution of this
work.
\end{finding}

\begin{finding}{F3 --- The manifold assumption holds only partially, and I measured it rather than assumed it.}
The polar split is worthwhile only if the radii are close to uninformative. On
real tokens I measured a relative radius spread of $8.2\%$ (an isotropic
Gaussian would give $\approx\!1.7\%$), of which only $\approx\!7\%$ is
positional --- leaving $\approx\!8\%$ genuinely content-dependent --- and a
pairwise direction overlap of $|\cos| = 0.35$ uncentred, falling to $0.17$ after
centring.
\emph{Consequence:} the radius side-model must be a real model, so I made it a
per-token cross-attention head; and centring is load-bearing, which is why
\S\ref{sec:open} treats it as an open measurement rather than a settled choice.
\end{finding}

\begin{finding}{F4 --- The curved formulation earns its keep on stability, not only on accuracy.}
At matched epochs the spherical model leads the flat Euclidean baseline on the
semantic axis, and the flat baseline diverged outright (\texttt{NaN} collapse at
epoch 60; its best checkpoint scored $0.082 / 0.205 / 0.712$).
\emph{Consequence:} the geometry is retained. Notably, it also dissolves two
correctness hazards of the flat formulation --- guidance anisotropy under
standardisation, and the temptation to normalise the sequence.
\end{finding}

\begin{finding}{F5 --- The decoder is not the bottleneck.}
Decoding \emph{ground-truth} tokens reaches PixCorr $0.638$ on the full 982
images. My prior's anchor sits at a mean token cosine of $\approx\!0.37$.
\emph{Consequence:} all remaining headroom is in the prior. This is the single
measurement that determined everything after it.
\end{finding}

\begin{finding}{F6 --- The low-level pathway fails from both directions, which indicts the target.}
MindEye2 obtains its pixel scores by fusing a semantic pathway with a low-level
one that supplies the sampler's initialisation. I implemented the same mechanism
four times: mean-pooled tokens; a spatial token grid; a head reading raw voxels;
and the same with $L_1$ on a lower-resolution target. The first three plateaued
at exactly the score of predicting the per-image mean colour, and a $17.4$M head
scored identically to a $1.5$M one ($0.0699$ vs.\ $0.0702$) --- so capacity was
never the constraint. The fourth removed the mean-seeking behaviour and
overfitted instead, validation error rising monotonically $0.083\!\to\!0.104$.
\emph{Consequence:} underfitting and overfitting failing on the same objective
indicts the \emph{target}, not the model. A $49$k-dimensional RGB image is not a
learnable intermediate from $27$k trials. This result also supplies the first
half of my argument in \S\ref{sec:pixcorr}.
\end{finding}

\begin{finding}{F7 --- Capacity is the wrong lever.}
Widening the encoder $2048\!\to\!4096$ produced $0.091 / 0.233 / 0.748$ and
overfitted from the \emph{first} evaluation: validation token cosine fell
monotonically $0.3485\!\to\!0.3053$ across 150 epochs.
\emph{Consequence:} the model is data-starved, not capacity-starved. Any
remaining gain must come from better conditioning of the existing problem.
\end{finding}

\begin{finding}{F8 --- The generative model was being started from a strictly uninformative point.}
This is the observation the present work turns on. The flow transported a
uniform sample $z_0\sim\mathrm{Unif}(\Sph{d-1})$ onto the target. In $d=1664$
dimensions, $\langle z_0,z_1\rangle \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1/d)$, so the source is
$\approx\!90^\circ$ from \emph{every} target and equidistant from all of them:
it carries no information whatsoever. Meanwhile my regression anchor $\mu(c)$
already stood at $\cos\approx0.37$.
\emph{Consequence:} the transport was being asked to begin strictly behind a
quantity I already possessed. Transporting from the anchor instead
(\S\ref{sec:source}) makes $v\equiv0$ the identity map, so the prior is
\emph{bounded below} by the anchor and can only refine it.
\end{finding}

\begin{finding}{F9 --- The training objective was mis-allocating capacity across time.}
Having fixed the source, I re-derived the objective and found the velocity
parametrisation ill-conditioned: it implicitly weights endpoint error by
$1/(1-t)^2$, concentrating capacity where the problem is trivial and starving
$t\!\to\!0$, which is where inference begins (\S\ref{sec:param}).
\emph{Consequence:} predict the endpoint, not the velocity.
\end{finding}

\begin{finding}{F10 --- No discriminative signal reached the decoder's input.}
The contrastive term acted on a pooled auxiliary projection that the decoder
never sees. Every one of the $425{,}984$ numbers the decoder consumes was
supervised by mean-seeking regression alone --- the same condition that produced
the collapses in F6.
\emph{Consequence:} apply the contrastive loss to the flattened predicted token
grid (\S\ref{sec:contrastive}).
\end{finding}

\noindent
F1--F7 establish that the prior is the bottleneck and that neither capacity nor
pixel-space supervision will move it. F8--F10 are the response, and together
they define the method below.

\section{The resulting method}
\label{sec:method}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{Polar reparametrisation onto the oblique manifold}

I split each token about a fixed mean,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:polar}
X_i = \mu + r_i z_i,\qquad
r_i = \lVert X_i-\mu\rVert \in \R_+,\qquad
z_i = \tfrac{X_i-\mu}{r_i} \in \Sph{d-1},\qquad d=1664 .
\end{equation}
The map is a bijection, so the decoder still receives exact raw tokens. The
directions live on the \emph{oblique manifold} $\mathcal{M}=(\Sph{d-1})^{N}$,
$N=256$, and are modelled by Riemannian flow matching~\cite{chen2024riemannian};
the radii are handled by a light side-model. I state plainly that this is an
\emph{imposed} reparametrisation rather than native geometry --- F3 is its
empirical justification.

I further factor the sequence against the pooled embedding
$c_{\mathrm{cls}}\in\Sph{1279}$, a near-complete semantic summary:
$p(X\mid c) = p(c_{\mathrm{cls}}\mid c)\, p(X\mid c, c_{\mathrm{cls}})$, sampling
the global direction first so that it steers local detail.

\subsection{Geodesic transport and its singularities}

For endpoints separated by $\theta=\arccos\langle z_0,z_1\rangle$ the conditional
path is the great-circle interpolant, with target velocity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slerpvel}
u_t=\frac{\theta}{\sin\theta}\Big(\!-\cos\big((1-t)\theta\big)z_0+\cos(t\theta)z_1\Big)
\in\Tang{z_t}\Sph{d-1},
\qquad \lVert u_t\rVert=\theta .
\end{equation}
Outputs are tangent-projected and integration uses the exponential map, so
iterates stay on $\mathcal{M}$ exactly. Three obstacles required care:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Removable singularities at both ends.} The $1/\sin\theta$ factor is
      singular for coincident \emph{and} antipodal endpoints. Both limits exist;
      a naive implementation returns $0/0$ and propagates \texttt{NaN} through
      the batch. I guard each expression on its degenerate set and substitute the
      analytic limit.
\item \textbf{Conditioning of the logarithmic map.} Writing
      $\theta=\arccos\langle z,y\rangle$ needs the argument clamped away from
      $\pm1$, and that clamp puts a \emph{floor} under $\Log_z(y)$: at $y=z$ it
      returns a small vector in an arbitrary direction. Harmless when $\Log$
      merely measures a displacement, fatal in \S\ref{sec:param} where it is
      evaluated at coincidence and integrated. The stable form,
      $\theta=\operatorname{atan2}(\lVert\Proj_z y\rVert, \langle z,y\rangle)$,
      is exact at $\theta=0$, well-conditioned near $\pi$, and needs no clamp.
\item \textbf{Out-of-distribution query times.} Training samples $t$ from a
      logit-normal law with almost no mass at the endpoints, yet a naive
      integrator evaluates the field at exactly $t\!=\!0,1$. On a manifold this
      is worse than in flat space: a bad velocity can push the iterate where the
      retraction is ill-conditioned. I mix uniform $t$ into training and clamp
      query times.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{An informative source, and how to calibrate it}
\label{sec:source}

Following F8, the source becomes a \emph{wrapped Gaussian} posterior centred on
the regression anchor:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:posterior}
q(z_0\mid y)=\Exp_\mu\big(\Proj_\mu\varepsilon\big),\qquad
\varepsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 I_d).
\end{equation}
Every point of the path then carries brain information, and $v\equiv0$ is the
identity flow. Two subtleties, both high-dimensional in origin:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The width must not match the anchor error.} A draw is displaced in
      a \emph{random} tangent direction while the target sits in one specific
      direction; for large $d$ these are near-orthogonal and
      $\cos(z_0,z_1)\approx\cos\phi\,\cos\theta$, with $\phi$ the displacement.
      At the measured operating point ($\cos\theta=0.37$, $\theta=68.3^\circ$),
      setting $\phi=\theta$ would drive $\cos(z_0,z_1)$ to $0.126$ --- worse
      than the anchor itself. Setting $\phi=0.15$\,rad costs $0.370\to0.366$,
      i.e.\ nothing measurable, while still giving the field a neighbourhood on
      which to be defined.
\item \textbf{Angles, not coordinate variances.} For an isotropic tangent
      Gaussian $\E\lVert\Proj_\mu\varepsilon\rVert\approx\sigma\sqrt{d-1}$, and
      on the sphere that norm \emph{is} the distance travelled; hence
      $\sigma=\phi/\sqrt{d-1}$. A width specified as a raw $\sigma$ silently
      changes meaning with the token dimension --- a factor of
      $\sqrt{1663}\approx41$ here. Every angular hyper-parameter is therefore
      expressed in radians and converted internally.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What may carry gradient.} F1's lemma applies directly. If the flow
loss pushes on the base point $\mu$, the model shortens its own transport by
degrading the anchor. If nothing in the source carries gradient, the width is
fixed by its prior and the variational structure is decorative. The correct
asymmetry is: \emph{base point detached} (it is supervised by its own cosine
loss), \emph{width trainable}. The flow then pulls $\sigma$ down and a
Kullback--Leibler term floors it; per tangent dimension,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{KL}=\tfrac12\Big[\tfrac{\sigma^2}{\sigma_p^2}-1+2\log\tfrac{\sigma_p}{\sigma}\Big],
\qquad
\frac{\partial\,\mathrm{KL}}{\partial\log\sigma}=\frac{\sigma^2}{\sigma_p^2}-1,
\end{equation}
whose unique stationary point is $\sigma=\sigma_p$. The equilibrium between that
floor and the flow's downward pressure makes the posterior a genuine model of
anchor uncertainty.

\paragraph{Guidance becomes ill-posed.} Classifier-free guidance contrasts a
conditional velocity against an unconditional one. When $z_0$ is itself
brain-derived, the ``unconditional'' branch still starts from a brain-dependent
point; the branches do not differ in the way guidance assumes. I therefore set
the guidance scale to $1$ under an informative source.

\subsection{Endpoint rather than velocity parametrisation}
\label{sec:param}

This follows from an identity, not an experiment. For the geodesic path,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:identity}
u_t=\frac{\Log_{z_t}(z_1)}{1-t},
\end{equation}
verified numerically at $d=1664$ to $4\times10^{-8}$--$2\times10^{-6}$, i.e.\
float32 round-off. The two parametrisations describe the \emph{same} field; only
the loss geometry differs. Substituting into the velocity mean-squared error,
\begin{equation}
\lVert v_\theta-u_t\rVert^2
=\frac{\lVert\Log_{z_t}(\hat z_1)-\Log_{z_t}(z_1)\rVert^2}{(1-t)^2},
\end{equation}
so training on velocity weights \emph{endpoint} error by $1/(1-t)^2$. Two
consequences, both measured:

\begin{center}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccc@{}}
\toprule
$t$ & $0.1$ & $0.5$ & $0.9$ & $0.98$ \\
implicit endpoint weight $1/(1-t)^2$ & $1.2\times$ & $4\times$ & $100\times$ & $2500\times$ \\
\midrule
anchor error $\theta$ & $90.0^\circ$ & $68.3^\circ$ & $30.0^\circ$ & $15.0^\circ$ \\
(cosine loss)\,/\,(velocity loss) & $674\times$ & $738\times$ & $813\times$ & $827\times$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The first row is backwards: near $t\!=\!1$ recovering $z_1$ from $z_t$ is nearly
trivial, while near $t\!=\!0$ the inversion is ill-conditioned and the model must
genuinely use the brain signal --- and $t\!=\!0$ is where inference starts. The
second follows from $\lVert u_t\rVert=\theta$ in \eqref{eq:slerpvel}: since the
mean-squared error averages over $d$ coordinates, the flow term sits three orders
of magnitude below its neighbours, and the imbalance \emph{tightens} as the model
improves.

\paragraph{Resolution.} The network emits a residual toward the endpoint,
\begin{equation}
\hat z_1=\frac{z_t+f_\theta(z_t,t,c)}{\lVert z_t+f_\theta(z_t,t,c)\rVert},
\qquad
v_\theta=\frac{\Log_{z_t}(\hat z_1)}{1-t},
\end{equation}
trained on $1-\cos(\hat z_1,z_1)$, uniform in $t$. The $1/(1-t)^2$ re-weighting
disappears; the flow term acquires the same natural scale as the anchor loss
(measured ratio $\approx\!7$ rather than $\approx\!1/740$), making their relative
weight an interpretable hyper-parameter; and because the output layer is
zero-initialised, $\hat z_1 = z_t$ at initialisation, so $v\equiv0$ and the
identity-flow bound of F8 holds \emph{by construction}.

\subsection{Discriminative supervision on the decoder path}
\label{sec:contrastive}

Following F10, the contrastive loss is additionally applied to the flattened
$256\times1664$ predicted grid rather than to a pooled projection. Pooling would
discard precisely the per-position structure the term exists to supervise.
Contrastive objectives are the natural counterweight to mean-seeking regression
because they are \emph{relative}: a prediction resembling the dataset average
scores at chance against in-batch distractors, however good its cosine to its own
target.

\subsection{Exposure bias in the two-head factorisation}

The patch head sees the true $c_{\mathrm{cls}}$ during training and a sampled
estimate at inference. Since $c_{\mathrm{cls}}$ nearly determines the image, a
head given it for free learns to route around the brain tokens. I treat this as
scheduled sampling and support teacher forcing, a geodesic rotation of the truth
by a calibrated angle, and true sampled conditioning. The rotation angle is
calibrated directly from the logged agreement between the sampled and true global
direction --- another place where expressing perturbations as angles rather than
coordinate variances is necessary rather than merely tidy.

\section{Why PixCorr is not a sound optimisation target}
\label{sec:pixcorr}
% ===========================================================================

I report PixCorr because the literature does, but I do not let it drive design.
Three independent arguments agree.

\paragraph{1. Theory: distortion versus perception.}
PixCorr is a squared-error criterion, uniquely maximised by the posterior mean
$\E[x\mid y]$. Under fMRI conditioning that posterior is broad --- the
measurement does not determine high-frequency content --- so $\E[x\mid y]$ is a
\emph{blur}: not a plausible image, yet the PixCorr optimum. Conversely a model
sampling correctly from $p(x\mid y)$ incurs roughly twice the posterior mean's
squared error. This is the perception--distortion
trade-off~\cite{blau2018perception}: \textbf{a perfect generative model is
penalised relative to a blur}. Optimising PixCorr selects against the behaviour I
want --- and it is exactly the failure observed four times in F6.

\paragraph{2. Measurement: PixCorr scores the initialisation, not the decoding.}
Holding the semantic content fixed at ground truth and varying only how much
low-frequency information the sampler is handed:

\begin{center}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Decoder input (semantics identical throughout)} & \textbf{PixCorr} & \textbf{SSIM} & \textbf{CLIP} \\
\midrule
GT tokens, noise initialisation (full 982)  & $0.638$ & $0.249$ & --- \\
GT tokens, GT-blur init, strength $1.0$     & $0.730$ & $0.353$ & $\approx1.0$ \\
GT tokens, GT-blur init, strength $0.7$     & $0.870$ & $0.444$ & $\approx1.0$ \\
GT tokens, GT-blur init, strength $0.5$     & $0.913$ & $0.478$ & $\approx1.0$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

PixCorr moves $0.638\to0.913$ on initialisation alone. It is therefore largely a
measure of the sampler's starting point, not of what was decoded from the brain.

\paragraph{3. Operation: it trades against semantics on a decode-time dial.}
One checkpoint, full 982 images, two decode configurations:

\begin{center}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Decode configuration} & \textbf{PixCorr} & \textbf{SSIM} & \textbf{CLIP-2way} \\
\midrule
regression point estimate, guidance $1.0$ & $\mathbf{0.164}$ & $\mathbf{0.348}$ & $0.703$ \\
blended prior sample, guidance $1.0$      & $0.131$ & $0.276$ & $\mathbf{0.738}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The same weights give either the better pixel score or the better semantic score,
with no retraining. A PixCorr figure quoted without its paired semantic figure is
not a property of the model. Note also that the low-variance point estimate ---
the least generative option --- wins on PixCorr, exactly as argument 1 predicts.

\paragraph{What I use instead.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Ranking statistics} (two-way identification, $k$-way retrieval).
      Writing a predictor as $\hat x = a\,x + b\,\bar m + e$ (signal, shared
      component, idiosyncratic error), a pointwise criterion is increasing in the
      shared component $b$ whereas a centred pairwise criterion is invariant to
      it. Ranking statistics cannot be gamed by predicting the dataset average
      --- the specific failure mode of F6.
\item \textbf{Angular fidelity added over the anchor.} Because the transport now
      begins at the anchor, the gain of the flow over its own starting point is
      directly measurable, and isolates the generative component from the
      regression baseline. It needs no diffusion pass, so I log it on both splits
      at every evaluation; the train/validation gap then separates underfitting
      from overfitting per component.
\item \textbf{Selection on a ranking statistic.} I previously selected
      checkpoints on a pointwise token cosine and observed it move \emph{opposite}
      to decoded semantic quality over a hundred epochs of one run. Selection now
      uses the two-way statistic on the quantity the decoder receives.
\end{itemize}

\noindent
This is not an argument that low-level fidelity is unimportant. It is an argument
that PixCorr is the wrong instrument for it, and that the correct route is
structural --- a working low-level pathway supplying the sampler's initialisation
--- not tuning a semantic model against a criterion it cannot satisfy without
becoming a blur.

\section{Standing and the immediate question}
\label{sec:open}
% ===========================================================================

\begin{center}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Method} & \textbf{PixCorr} $\uparrow$ & \textbf{SSIM} $\uparrow$ & \textbf{CLIP-2way} $\uparrow$ \\
\midrule
Ours --- regression point estimate      & $0.164$ & $0.348$ & $0.703$ \\
Ours --- blended prior sample           & $0.131$ & $0.276$ & $0.738$ \\
Ours --- single-subject (earlier stage) & ---     & ---     & $\approx0.82$ \\
\midrule
Takagi \& Nishimoto 2023~\cite{takagi2023}   & $0.246$ & $0.410$ & $0.821$ \\
Ozcelik \& VanRullen 2023~\cite{ozcelik2023} & $0.273$ & $0.365$ & $0.909$ \\
MindEye2~\cite{scotti2024mindeye2}           & $0.322$ & $0.431$ & $0.930$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
I am below the published comparisons at any single operating point, and the gap
is concentrated exactly where F6 predicts: I have no working low-level pathway,
while MindEye2's pixel scores come from fusing one with its semantic pathway.

\paragraph{Established.} The curved formulation trains stably where the flat
baseline diverges and leads it on the semantic axis (F4). The decoder is not the
bottleneck (F5). Capacity is not the lever (F7).

\paragraph{The one open question.} Whether the transport --- now started from an
informative source (F8) and trained under a well-conditioned objective (F9) ---
improves on its own anchor. This is a single scalar, it is now directly
observable, and the current experiment is designed around nothing else.

\paragraph{Two caveats I am not yet in a position to close.}
First, the centring vector is a single element of $\R^{1664}$ averaged over
images \emph{and} over all $256$ positions; if bigG tokens carry a
position-specific common mode, each centred direction retains a component that is
identical across images and predictable without any brain data, yet counted in
full by every cosine I report. Per-position centring and the diagnostic that
sizes this effect are implemented but not yet run, so my anchor cosines should be
read as upper bounds. Second, every recent run is a four-subject joint model
using per-subject input projections over a shared trunk, while my earlier
single-subject baseline was substantially stronger; a single-subject control is
required before attributing the difference to the prior.
