\section*{Answers to the questions in the chat, 5:50\,pm}

\textbf{The power law.} The $x$-axis is the relative Frobenius size of the violation: for the
span condition, $\|P_\perp(C+C^{\T})\|_F/\|C+C^{\T}\|_F$ with $P_\perp$ removing the components
along $\Sigma_0$ and $\Sigma_1$; for the skew condition, $\|\mathrm{skew}(C)\|_F/\|C\|_F$. The
$y$-axis is the relative displacement of the endpoint map,
$\|M(C)-M_0\|_F/\|M_0\|_F$. Fitted exponents over $180$ points per family: $0.983$
(rms residual $0.035$) for span and $0.942$ (rms $0.056$) for skew --- both unity within the
fit.

\textbf{The $6.6\times$, now derived.} The ratio is the quotient of two response constants, and
the mechanism is: the span response carries two suppression factors the skew response does not
--- a window $t(1-t)$ vanishing at both endpoints, and the rate at which the two interpolant
variances separate. With equal marginals every symmetric perturbation produces exactly zero
first-order response while the skew response has a closed form. The ratio is therefore
approximately constant across violation sizes (both responses are linear) but is \emph{not} a
universal constant across settings: it grows as the marginals become similar, running from $76.8$
to $7.8$ as one marginal is interpolated toward the other, so $6.585$ is the sampled ensemble's
value at its own separation. Full note with proofs and six numerical checks attached
(\texttt{ratio.tex}).

\textbf{The loss floor.} It is the Markovization gap
$\int_0^1\mathbb E[\operatorname{Tr}\operatorname{Var}(U_t\mid X_t)]\,dt$, the irreducible floor
of the flow-matching loss. Invariance of the endpoint map under the stated conditions is proved
(the pencil criterion); the floor's failure to track the map is both proved and measured: on
couplings provably equivalent at the endpoint the floor spans a median factor $2.23$, while on
couplings that move the map by a median $63\%$ it spans only $1.23$. Endpoint differences are
relative Frobenius norms.

\textbf{New since yesterday.} The criterion also holds on a pretrained image-to-image model we
did not train. Stable Diffusion v1.5, STL-10 photographs degraded by downsample--upsample,
scored by CLIP alignment to the clean image, seven transport strengths, three seeds:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrr@{}}
\toprule
degradation factor & source alignment & best gain from transporting\\
\midrule
$2$  & $0.997$ & $-0.189$\\
$4$  & $0.853$ & $-0.061$\\
$8$  & $0.733$ & $-0.051$\\
$16$ & $0.430$ & $+0.115$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Transporting hurts at $2\times$, $4\times$ and $8\times$ degradation and starts to pay only
between $8\times$ and $16\times$, i.e.\ once the source's alignment with the target falls below
roughly $0.64$. This is $64$ images, one checkpoint and one degradation family --- a targeted
test rather than a broad validation.
